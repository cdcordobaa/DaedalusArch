## 3. Hand-written violation analogues (independent of these proxies)

The reference projects' violation variants were written before this unit. Three of them are analogues of a batch-2 rule. None of the 52 violation variants gets a new batch-2 flag against its clean base (`scores.json`, `violations`). For the three analogues:

| Variant | Rule it is an analogue of | Batch-2 flag | Why not |
|---|---|---|---|
| `hexagonal/v04-use-case-throws-http-error` (`CancelBookingService` throws the inbound adapter's `HttpError(404)`) | error-flow `framework-http-exception-in-core` | no | `HttpError` is a project class in the HTTP adapter, not a framework package exception. The rule's catalogue does not cover an outer-layer project error type. |
| `hexagonal/v05-inbound-adapter-reads-outbound-adapter` (`RoomController` reads `InMemoryRoomRepository`) | reachability `endpoint-bypasses-core` | no | The reference controllers are framework-free classes, not Nest routes or express handlers, so they are not `http` entries. The bypass rule only runs from http and message entries. |
| `layered/v03-controller-reads-gateway` (`CatalogController` reads `ProductGateway`) | reachability `endpoint-bypasses-core` | no | Same reason. |

**Catch on the independent analogues: 0/3.**

**Possible extensions.** Each miss has a natural extension:

- error flow: treat an error type declared in an infrastructure- or presentation-kind file, thrown by the core, as a transport error;
- reachability: treat the public methods of a `*Controller` class in a presentation- or infrastructure-kind layer as `http` entries.

**Not applied.** These changes would be made *after* seeing these three analogues, which would spend the only independent evidence. They are recorded as future work, to be validated on held-out projects.

Findings:

- **Loop demo.** The stub plants one domain → infrastructure import at step 2 (`src/domain/tags.ts` → `src/infrastructure/tags-store.ts`).
  - Both the harness's recorded `STUB-DEP` finding and the in-memory `dependency-direction` rule appear as new at `step-02` and are attributed to `step-02` through the IMPORTS edge.
  - In the `gate` arm, the repair turn `step-02-repair` resolves both: −2.
  - In `none` and `daedalus-mcp`, both findings persist to step 3. This is the erosion the Study 2 slope measures, here broken down by the step that introduced it.
  - The stub's per-step services share a shape but differ in concept, and no REIMPLEMENTS candidate is raised.
- **Synthetic history.** The three planted re-implementations are found: two clones and one by shared concept and vocabulary.
  - step-02 `TagService.overdueTagged` clones `TodoService.overdue` (step-01);
  - step-03 `TodoStore` re-states `InMemoryTodoRepository` (step-01);
  - step-04 `isBehind` clones `isLate` (step-01).

  The step-03 domain → infrastructure import is new at step-03 and attributed to it by edge.
- **Attribution fix.** In the first run, the advisory clone and shared-literal pairs of step-04 were attributed half to step-01, through the old end of each pair. A two-ended finding is now attributed to the later of its two ends, the step that made the pair. Its unit lookup also covers Function nodes. The synthetic test asserts this. This change was made on the synthetic fixture, not on reference data.
- **Locality.** At step-04 the turn touched 3 components, but its scope is 2 (it added files in `todos` and `reports` and edited `tags`), so locality = 0.67. In the layer-first demo every step's components are layers, and locality is 1 by construction (design-note limit).
