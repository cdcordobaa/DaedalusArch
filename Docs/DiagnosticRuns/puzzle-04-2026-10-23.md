by the agent through MCP), does the clean-solve rate rise above the agent-alone baselines on the puzzles where the agent
alone falls into the trap? Per puzzle, never pooled.

## 5. Reading (dev, n = 5 per puzzle × arm)

- **Where the checker sees the trap, both delivery modes work.** On P6 both arms go from 0/5 to 5/5. On P5 both arms
  remove the trap from the oracle's view in 4–5 of 5 runs. The registered count stays at 0 only because of a request
  defect that is not about architecture.
- **The gate catches the trap at the edit that introduces it,** and the agents act on the message within the same
  turn. Over 25 sessions:
  - 28 keys were shown, and 28 were resolved by the end of their session (P1 5, P2 2, P5 16, P6 5);
  - 0 were left open on a final tree.
  - Most keys were resolved in the turn that created them. Only the three P1 `saveAll` sessions carried one into a
    step snapshot, and there the oracle still fails at the end on the leftover port. So snapshot-grain
    caught-and-fixed is 0.
- **The pull arm changes the design before any finding.** P5 under pull: 4 of 5 sessions never got a finding and wrote
  the relay design anyway. On P6 the pull agents also cleaned up planted debt the gate never showed. Tool + instruction
  is not the same treatment as the gate (caveat §7).
- **The remaining non-clean gate trees are oracle edge cases, not missed traps,** with one exception (P5 c07).
  - P1-SG1-c and P2-SG1-e are new over-rejection candidates.
  - P2-SG2-a is the known one.
  - The exception, P5 c07, moved a cross-context write into the composition root, where `check_consistency` does not
    look. It is the first case of the plan's gaming concern seen in an agent tree: the checker is silent and the
    oracle fails. It was not a response to a finding: in that session the gate never showed a key on the cancel path.
- **The blind control behaves as a control.** On P3r the gate said nothing and changed nothing measurable, so the P1,
  P2, P5 and P6 effects are not a general effect of the hooks being on.
- **Not a separation, except P6.** At n = 5, 2/5 vs 0/5 or 1/5 is a direction (R5). The P1 and P2 comparisons with
  PUZZLE-02 rest on the hand read.
