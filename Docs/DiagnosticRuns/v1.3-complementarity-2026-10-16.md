# Symbolic–neural complementarity matrix on the planted seeds (N2), 2026-10-16

> **DEVELOPMENT-SET NUMBERS.** Every seed comes from the judge catch rounds 1, 2 and DUP v2 (the v1.3 reference projects, the `antipattern-nest` fixture and the E1 cells), plus the CHECK-2 bases of the unmerged `v1.3-judge-v22-prep` branch. All of it is development data. The figures describe **which planted seeds the rules can see at all, and how the judge did on each kind**. They are not validity estimates. There is no pre-registration. No LLM call was made: every judge verdict is read from committed score and verdict files.

Branch `v1.3-complementarity-matrix`, cut from `origin/v1.3` @ `d65fddbb`. Script: `scripts/v1.3/complementarity.ts`. Outputs: `results/v1.3-complementarity/` (`rows.csv`, `clean-units.csv`, `scores.json`, `scores.md`, `symbolic.json`).

## 0. The rule, committed before any number (`cb9f16a6`; that commit's `--no-e1` edit to two frozen seed scripts is reverted on this branch, §1)

- **Symbolic findings.** The report's violations, minus the neural functions (FF-N*). Each takes the tier the report's `tiers` table gives its function: blocking or advisory. Any other function counts as `none` and is ignored.
- **New findings.** The seeded copy's findings minus the base's, as a multiset over `functionId|filePath|target`. A second occurrence of a key the base has once counts as new.
- **Symbolic-visible.** At least one new blocking or advisory finding sits on an edited or created file of the seed, or names one as its target (the seeded edge; the target may be written with or without its extension). Otherwise the seed is **symbolic-blind**.
- **Twins.** Classified by the same rule. A "visible" twin is a symbolic alarm on a near-miss.
- **Clean units.** A clean unit is **symbolic-pass** when the unmodified base has no blocking finding on any of the unit's files. The unit's files are the `filePaths` that the recorded judge report lists for it.
- **Symbolic run.** `firewall evaluate --symbolic-only --engine cypher-interp --scoring v1.3` on every base and copy.
- **Judge columns.** Read from the round scores:
  - v1 `catch`: unit fail or construct-flag majority. This is the rounds' headline.
  - v1 `unitFail`.
  - v2.1 majority unit fail, from `results/v1.3-judge-v2/{tune,check}-v2.1`. The rubric was tuned on TUNE, so TUNE results are in-sample.
- **Pooling.** Seeds are de-duplicated by base and normalised committed diff, keeping the first in round order r1, r2, dv2. All 9 DUP v2 seeds turned out to be byte-identical edits of round 1 and round 2 DUP-RULE seeds, with the same judge outcome and the same class.
- **Bug fix after the first run.** One fix to input reading was made after the first run (`base:` to `base__` in the recorded-report file name). It made the run fail, so no number had been computed. The rule did not change.

## 3. The cost side

**Clean units.** Unlike the seeds, the clean units were first counted per round. Twelve (base, function, unit) triples are judged in both r2 and dv2. They are now also counted once, as a distinct unit that fails when any round failed it. In every one of the 12, both rounds gave the same verdict. This de-duplicated count was added at review on PR #179.
- **De-duplicated (headline): judge false fail given symbolic-pass: 2/48 = 0.04 [0.01, 0.14].** There are 50 distinct units and 48 are symbolic-pass. The two failing units:
  - `e1/claude-haiku-4-5…minimal-prose.run-2` `src/application/usecases`;
  - `e1/claude-sonnet-5-5…full-aac.run-1` `src/domain`.
- Per round, as first reported: 3/60 = 0.05 [0.02, 0.14] (62 judged, 60 symbolic-pass). The haiku unit counts twice, once in r2 and once in dv2.
- The 2 clean units with a blocking finding are the two `antipattern-nest` entity files, where FF-P01 flags a `typeorm` import. The judge fails both: 2/2.
- v2.1 false fail given symbolic-pass:
  - TUNE: 6/36 = 0.17 [0.08, 0.32];
  - CHECK: 2/24 = 0.08 [0.02, 0.26].
- **No clean unit is free of advisory findings** (0 of 62). An "advisory-quiet" control does not exist in this set.

**Twins** (55 judged near-misses):
- symbolic alarm on 0/55 = 0.00 [0.00, 0.07];
- judge (v1 unit fail) false fail on 10/55 = 0.18 [0.10, 0.30].

So on the near-misses the rules never fire and the judge does, in about one case in five.

## 6. What this does not show

- **Validity.** It is not a validity result. The judge's catch on symbolic-blind seeds is construct sensitivity on textbook, planted anti-patterns in development projects. It says nothing about real-world defects or about precision on unplanted code.
- **The headline sample.** The 26 symbolic-blind seeds come from two places only:
  - one fixture (10);
  - the E1 cells (16), where the symbolic findings are recorded, not re-run.
  
  Nine of the 26 are DUP-RULE seeds, which the round-2 note treats as confounded. Without them the blind catch is 8/12 on one construct (FRAMEWORK-LEAK), and its lower bound is 0.39.
- **"Structurally cannot see."** Symbolic-blind means *the v1.3 rules as configured for that spec did not flag the seeded file*. It does not mean no rule could. The visible/blind split follows the stratum (hand-planted vs operator-planted) and the spec's layer kinds (see CHECK-2 FRAMEWORK-LEAK), as much as the construct.
- **The cost side.** It is small: 48 distinct symbolic-pass clean units and 55 twins. The twin false-fail rate includes the DUP-RULE twins that the round-2 note flags as cue-confounded.
- **Construct switches.** None were used or computed (Q1–Q25 status quo). No Study 2 material was read or derived.

## 7. Reproduce

```
npx tsx scripts/v1.3/complementarity.ts prepare --work <work>            # frozen seed scripts; shadow root if no E1 outcomes
# optional CHECK-2: in a read-only checkout of origin/v1.3-judge-v22-prep
npx tsx scripts/v1.3/judge-v22-check2.ts seed --out <work>/c2 --check
npx tsx scripts/v1.3/complementarity.ts --work <work> [--check2-repo <v22 checkout>]
```

Two runs from scratch here gave byte-identical `rows.csv`, `clean-units.csv`, `scores.json` and `symbolic.json`. Wall time: about 1 minute to prepare and 2 minutes to score (about 130 interpreter evaluations, no Neo4j). On a machine with `../daedalus-e1-outcomes`, `prepare` re-seeds the E1 rows too. The scorer still reads their findings from the committed reports. To re-run them on the interpreter, drop the `isE1` fallback in `main`; that is a later choice, not made here.
