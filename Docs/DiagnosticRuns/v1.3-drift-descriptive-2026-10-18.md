# v1.3 drift in human project histories, descriptive (2026-10-18)

**Status: descriptive only, zero calls.** No model or API call, no Neo4j, no re-walk of any history. This is lane G of the 2026-10-18 sprint plan (the plan file is now `ops/03-build-list.md`, where C5 is line 36), part C5 of the instrument map ("C5 drift: findings across t0 ... tn (descriptive)", `ops/01-instrument-map.md:89`; status row `ops/01-instrument-map.md:180`). It describes what the tool's blocking findings do over time. It says nothing about why.

## Headline

1. **Blocking findings accumulate.** Leave out FF-S02 cycles, whose count is capped at 100. Summed over 36 histories, blocking findings go from **404 at the first valid sampled snapshot to 1,824 at the last**. They rise in 27 repositories, fall in 2 and stay flat in 6. One repository has a single snapshot.
2. **Most of that rise tracks growth, not density.** The file-count proxy grows from 1,740 to 9,174 files. A shift-share split of the +1,420 change gives **+976 (69%) to growth and +444 (31%) to findings per file**. Start each history where the pin-time spec already maps at least half the files to a layer, and the growth share is **81%** (+1,056 of +1,299). In the 18 histories with at least 300 commits, the growth share is **89%** (+886 of +991). Pooled density actually *falls*, from 0.232 to 0.199 blocking findings per file. The per-repository median density rises (0 → 0.120, or 0.039 → 0.121 from the layered start).
3. **Introductions outnumber removals 3 to 1 at the sampled commits.** Across 4,640 valid sampled commits, 802 blocking findings appear and 265 disappear. Of the 802 introductions, 448 (56%) land in bot or mass-change commits and 24 are rename-matched, which leaves **330 "new"**. Of the 265 disappearances, 106 are FILE-DELETED, 52 RENAMED, 19 MASS-OR-BOT, 7 RESOLVED-IN-PLACE, 7 OTHER and **74 FIXED-BY-DEV**. Forty-eight of those 74 come from a single commit (amplication PR #93, `Docs/DiagnosticRuns/v1.3-validity-fixes-mined-2026-10-16.md` §6).
4. **Over whole windows between grid points,** 2,785 blocking lineages are born and 1,288 end. That is 21.6 births and 10.0 endings per repository-year, over 129.1 repository-years. Of the endings, 42% (543) leave every named file in place.
5. **Survival.** A blocking edge-shaped finding has a Kaplan-Meier one-year survival of **0.709** (0.760 without cycles; FF-S02 0.373, median 150 days). For unflagged cross-layer import edges the figure is **0.746**. These reproduce the fix-mining survival table exactly (`Docs/DiagnosticRuns/v1.3-validity-fixes-mined-2026-10-16.md` §5a).

## 1. Definitions and identity

- **Blocking.** Tiers come from `src/scoring-engine/rule-tiers.ts`: FF-S01, S02, S04, P01, P03, P06, DS01, DS02 and DT01–DT04 are blocking, and FF-S03 is blocking only for the `layered` style. This matches the tier on every committed lineage (0 mismatches, `summary.json` `consistency.tierMismatchLineages`).
- **Grid.** The 200 (or fewer) sampled first-parent commits per repository. At each valid grid commit C, the blocking count is the sum of `childByRule` over blocking rules (`pairs.json.gz`). It equals the number of blocking lineages alive at that grid point in `lineages.json.gz` at all 4,658 valid snapshots (0 mismatches, `consistency.gridCountMismatchSnapshots`).
- **Identity.** Identity is the fix-mining finding key (rule, file, target). Disappearances use the walker's cause taxonomy, in this order: RENAMED (`git diff -M50%` rename source), FILE-DELETED, MASS-OR-BOT (bot author, or ≥100 / ≥25% files changed, or ≥20 / ≥10% renamed or deleted), RESOLVED-IN-PLACE, FIXED-BY-DEV, OTHER (`results/v1.3-fixes-mined/protocol.md`; `Docs/DiagnosticRuns/v1.3-validity-fixes-mined-2026-10-16.md` §1 item 4). Introductions carry no cause in the walker output, so this note splits them three ways by the same logic:
  - *in a bot/mass commit*: the pair's `bot` or `mass` flag;
  - *rename-matched*: one introduction of rule R is paired with one RENAMED disappearance of rule R in the same commit;
  - *new*: everything else.

  The rename match is per rule, not per path, so it is an upper bound on rename noise.
- **FF-S02 cap.** The cycle template returns at most 100 rows. This note follows `scripts/v1.3/fixes-mined-report.ts`. Pair-level FF-S02 rows are dropped when either side is at the cap. Grid lineages whose birth or end touches a capped point are dropped. Time series report FF-S02 separately and mark capped points; 1,181 of the 4,658 valid snapshots are capped.
- **Two window sizes.**
  - *Pair level*: one sampled commit, P→C, where the cause taxonomy applies.
  - *Grid window*: from one valid grid commit to the next, which takes in the unsampled commits between them and carries only `filesStay`, not the full taxonomy.

  Of the 2,785 grid-window births, 802 (29%) happen in a sampled commit itself.
- **Size proxy.** No per-snapshot file list or LOC is committed (§6). The proxy is the number of distinct file paths named by any lineage alive at a grid point: a finding of any tier, or an unflagged internal import edge. At the last grid point it is a median 0.998 of the walker's own `atLastSample.files` (range 0.959–1.000, 36 repositories, `summary.json` `fileProxyCheck`). That check exists only at the last grid point. Files with no import edge and no finding are missed, so the proxy is a lower bound. A layered-file proxy counts the endpoints of unflagged edges whose two files both sit in a spec layer.
- **Shift-share.** With B blocking findings, F files and d = B/F, the change from the first to the last snapshot with F > 0 is split as ΔB = d̄·ΔF (growth term) + F̄·Δd (density term), using the means of the two endpoints. The split is exact and descriptive. It is not a causal decomposition.

## 6. KLOC: not computed, and what it would cost

Neither the committed outputs nor the snapshot schema have LOC.

- The walker's per-snapshot record is `{tree, ok, files, layers, imports, deps, violations}` (`scripts/v1.3/fixes-mined-run.ts:146`).
- That record lives only in the snapshot cache, a scratch directory "never committed" (`scripts/v1.3/fixes-mined-run.ts:15`), and the cache is not on this machine.
- Even the cached file list carries paths, not line counts.

So findings per file uses the validated path-count proxy above, and **findings per KLOC needs a re-walk**. Following the lane rule, it was **not started**.

Estimate for a LOC-only re-walk (no pipeline, no Neo4j, no calls):

- **Work.** Re-clone the 36 histories, blobless and sparse to the analysis path, as the original walk did (`Docs/DiagnosticRuns/v1.3-validity-fixes-mined-2026-10-16.md` §9). At the 4,917 sampled commits (sum of `sampled` over `results/v1.3-fixes-mined/repos/*/walk.json`), stream the source blobs through `git cat-file --batch` and count lines. Lines are cached per blob id, so an unchanged file is counted once.
- **Time.** The original walk took 17,450 s wall in total (sum of `wallSeconds`). Most of that was pipeline evaluation (median 1.7 s per snapshot over 8,319 evaluations, §1 item 3 of the fix-mining doc), and a LOC pass skips all of it. The remaining cost is network fetch of the blobs, plus well under 1 s per snapshot of local work. Estimate: **about 1 to 2 hours wall, one worker**, dominated by clone and blob fetch for the large monorepos (budibase, directus, immich, outline, unleash). This is an estimate, not a measurement; clone time was not logged.
- **Memory and disk.** Memory under 0.5 GB (streaming, one repository at a time). Disk is a few GB of transient clones under a scratch directory, deleted per repository. The last snapshots alone hold 9,278 files (sum of `atLastSample.files`).
- **What it would add.** A per-KLOC density column next to the per-file one. Files and KLOC usually move together within a repository, so the growth-share figures are unlikely to change direction. That is a guess, and the re-walk would test it.

## 7. What this does not show

- **No cause.** Rising counts, the growth share and survival are descriptions. Nothing here says developers ignore findings, that findings are right or wrong, or that growth *causes* findings. Fix mining already showed that keeping a finding fits both "the finding is wrong for this project" and "the debt is tolerated", and it could not separate them (`Docs/DiagnosticRuns/v1.3-validity-fixes-mined-2026-10-16.md`, Verdict).
- **Not architectural erosion.** A blocking finding is a rule hit under a spec written at the pin. Its meaningfulness (C3m) has 0 labels so far (`ops/01-instrument-map.md:196`). "More findings" here means more rule hits, not worse architecture.
- **Spec anachronism.** Each spec describes the pin-time layout. Early snapshots often have no layered files, so low early counts partly reflect the spec not matching the early tree. The layered-start variant reduces this but does not remove it.
- **Grid resolution.** At most 200 sampled commits per history. Births and endings are placed at grid points, and only 29% of births are seen at the commit that caused them. The full cause taxonomy applies only at the sampled commit.
- **Introductions have no cause.** The walker classifies disappearances only. The bot/mass and rename-matched split of introductions is this note's own approximation, and the rename match is per rule, not per path.
- **The size proxy is a lower bound and only checked at the end.** It is checked against the walker's file count at the last grid point (median ratio 0.998), not at earlier points. LOC is absent (§6).
- **The FF-S02 cap hides cycle growth.** 1,181 snapshots sit at the 100-cycle cap, which is why headline figures leave FF-S02 out. Late FF-S02 counts are floors.
- **Sample.** The corpus is 36 TypeScript backends, mostly public, about half of them teaching repositories with short histories (18 below 300 commits). Pooled figures are dominated by about ten large histories. There are no intervals: the tables describe these histories and make no estimate for a population.
- **Not Study 2.** Drift is a separate exploratory study (`ops/02-defensibility-conditions.md:55`, row 4.8). Nothing here informs Study 2's registered outcome or its power.
