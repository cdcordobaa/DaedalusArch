## 1. Projects

**Selection rule, the same as wave 1:**
1. Public, with a permissive licence in a licence file at the pin (MIT, ISC, BSD, Apache).
2. A TypeScript backend that claims the style in its README or folder structure.
3. Pinned to the last commit before 2023-01-01 on the default branch.
4. 15 to 300 non-test, non-`.d.ts` `.ts` files under `src/` of the analysis root.
5. Not in the Study 1 corpus, not one of the 14 W1 wild repositories (PR #114), and not in wave 1 (PR #133).
6. Well-known references preferred.

Wave 2 adds one rule, made explicit because two pins needed it: when the pinned commit is a bot commit, the last human commit must predate ChatGPT (2022-11-30) and no `src/` file may change between the two. This generalises wave 1's treatment of pvarentsov-clean (§1 of the wave-1 report).

**Discovery differs from wave 1.** Wave 1 did not use the GitHub search API. Wave 2 did: the session's GitHub tool searched TypeScript repositories created before mid-2022, sorted by stars, plus web search. The search only proposed candidates; the inclusion rules above decided. 123 distinct candidates were probed by anonymous blobless clone. The rejections are grouped with reasons in `selection.json`. The main reasons were no licence file (52), GPL or AGPL (7), a last human commit after ChatGPT (4), more than 300 files (2), fewer than 15 files, and copies of wave-1 projects.

### Evidence that the code predates AI coding tools

- **ChatGPT (2022-11-30).** All 20 last human commits predate it.
  - 18 pins predate it as well.
  - The two exceptions are bot commits whose last human commit is earlier: typeplate (snyk-bot 2022-12-24, human 2021-12-25) and pezzetti-base-app (dependabot 2022-12-11, human 2022-07-26). For both, `git diff` shows no `src/` change between the human commit and the pin.
- **Copilot general availability (2022-06-21).** 13 of the 20 last human commits predate it. The 7 after it are jamielivingstone, adilsonfuxe, geekyants, saluki, monstarlab, pezzetti and talyssonoc, all from 2022-07 to 2022-10.
- **Copilot technical preview (2021-06-29).** 6 projects predate it: nutes (2018), mustodon (2019), quiz4devs, camps and staart (2021-02 to 03), and ts-seed-hexagonal (2021-06-10).
- **Long human histories.** talyssonoc starts in 2017-03 and has 13 human authors up to the pin; saluki 2018-05, geekyants 2018-08, ngduc 2018-09, staart 2019-04 with 4,400+ commits, typeplate 2019-08, pezzetti 2019-10.
- **Thin histories.** ts-seed-hexagonal is one import commit, and mustodon and nutes have 2 to 5 commits. Their dates are the only evidence.
- **Dates.** Author and committer dates agree for 18 pins. For remix-hexagonal and quiz4devs-backend they differ by minutes to hours (rebase or merge), and both dates are before 2023 (`selection.json` → `provenance`).
- **Residual risk is as in wave 1.** Copilot completions cannot be ruled out for code written from 2021-06 to 2022-12, and commit dates are what the committer recorded. Pooled over 35 projects, 25 last human commits predate Copilot GA and 12 predate the preview.

**Style-claim strength.**
- **Prose claim in the README (15 projects).** jamielivingstone, nutes, felixle, adilsonfuxe, typeplate, shihabmridha, hexapinod, ts-seed (title only), remix, quiz4devs, pezzetti, talyssonoc, camps and mustodon claim the style in prose, and saluki documents its own Nest structure.
- **Folder structure only (2).** geekyants and ngduc. These are the weakest claims in wave 2, like Talento90 in wave 1.
- **Framework and file layout (2).** staart and bank-server are identified as NestJS this way.
- **Products, not templates (3).** staart-api (SaaS backend), bank-server (banking demo app) and remix-hexagonal (todo app with sharing). The rest are templates, boilerplates or teaching projects. That reflects rule 6 and the population of style-claiming repositories.

### Errata to the rationale, found after the run

The specs are unchanged; these notes correct or complete the explanatory text.

1. **nutes-template-base extracts a second application.** The project's root `tsconfig.json` includes `example/activity-tracking/`, a full example micro-service. The extractor took its 68 files, which are unmapped by the spec. The rationale did not mention them. They carry no layer, so they reach no layer rule, but they do enter the metrics and the advisory test-pairing rule.
2. **talyssonoc-node-api declaration files.** The rationale foresaw that the `*.d.ts` value-object and repository globs might match nothing. They do: the `.d.ts` files are not graph nodes. The harness counts 55 tsc edges into them as `missing-in-apg:.d.ts`. FF-DT03 and the repository-interface side of the repository rules therefore check nothing on this project.
3. **felixle-node-core imports are only partly in the graph** (§4). The rationale noted the `baseUrl` imports. It did not foresee that the default extractor would treat them as external packages.
4. **talyssonoc-node-api `src/__tests__/`.** `outside_contexts` lists `_sharedKernel`, `_lib`, `_boot` and the root files, but not the top-level test-helper folder `src/__tests__/`. Its two files are the two FF-DS01 rows (§3).

## 3. Runs

- **Engine.**
  - `--engine cypher-interp` is on `v1.3` (PR #128 merged) and was used for 19 projects.
  - **bank-server did not finish under the interpreter.** It was killed after 25 minutes. The FF-S02 (`no-cyclic-deps`) variable-length cycle query is the cause: on typeplate, the next slowest project, FF-S02 alone took 17 s of a 34 s run, and bank-server has 100 cycles in the default graph.
  - bank-server was therefore run on a local Neo4j 5.26.24 community server with APOC 5.26.24 core, from the release tarballs and bound to 127.0.0.1. That is the wave-1 engine. It finished in 47 s.
- **Engine parity.** All 19 interpreter runs were repeated on the same Neo4j server. The repeat reports are in scratch and not committed. Violation ids and v1.3 verdicts were identical on 19 of 19, so mixing the two engines in this set changes no number.
- **Command:** `npx tsx bin/firewall.ts evaluate --project <clone>/<root> --spec results/v1.3-external-2/specs/<id>.yaml --symbolic-only --engine cypher-interp --format json`.
- **Defaults kept, the same as wave 1:**
  - v1.3 scoring (rule tiers), instrument v2, full graph mode, `<root>/tsconfig.json`.
  - The judge was not run.
  - No dependencies were installed.
  - The clones were untouched; `git status` was clean after every run.
- **Cost.**
  - The interpreter runs took 3.1 to 34.1 s each, 106 s in total; bank-server took 47 s on Neo4j.
  - Exit code 1 means a failing verdict, not an error.
  - Parse coverage is 100% everywhere.
  - Import resolution reports 0 unresolved on all 20. That hides the felixle gap (§4).

**Results:**
- **The literal view is not exact on two of 20 projects.** In wave 1 it was exact on 15 of 15. It never adds an edge: confirmation is 1.000 everywhere. Both gaps are edges it misses:

    - The report's `importResolution` shows 0 unresolved and 368 external, so the gap is silent.
    - Many of the missing edges cross layers (application to domain, exposes to application). felixle's 7 blocking and 297 advisory findings were therefore computed on a graph without them and are likely undercounts. **This is the most consequential new fidelity result.**
  - **ts-seed-hexagonal: 3 edges missing in both views.** They come from `typeof import('../util/env')` in type positions, a type-query import form the extractor does not read. The target `util/` is unmapped, so no layer rule is affected.
- **The default graph differs as in wave 1, only more.**

**Reading:**
- **A failing verdict is the norm, not a signal.** 30 of 35 human-written, style-claiming projects fail the v1.3 verdict, and every style fails 6 of 7.
  - A verdict-level false-alarm rate cannot be computed without labels.
  - If these projects are taken as reasonably conforming references, the verdict alone does not separate conforming from non-conforming code at this sample size. The finding-level labels decide.
  - The rise from 0.73 in wave 1 to 0.95 in wave 2 is within the overlap of the two intervals.

## 6. Limitations

- **One author for selection and specs, as in wave 1.** Both are an AI judgement (Claude-family) over human code. A blind human spec for the same pins would remove the residual same-vendor influence. Judgements that drive whole groups of findings are listed in §3.
- **Discovery used the GitHub search API**, unlike wave 1. That biases the pool toward starred, tagged repositories. The inclusion rules did not change.
- **Small and template-heavy.** Wave 2 has 18 to 226 files per project and 3 products. Results do not generalise to large production systems.
- **Pre-2023 is a proxy, not proof.** 7 last human commits fall after Copilot GA. Two pins are bot commits after ChatGPT whose `src/` is the earlier human tree.
- **Two engines.** bank-server ran on Neo4j because the interpreter did not finish FF-S02. The other 19 were checked for identical results on Neo4j, so the mix is benign here. The interpreter's cost on cycle-heavy graphs is a usability problem for `--engine cypher-interp` on real projects.
- **Silent extractor gaps.** felixle's `baseUrl` bare imports and ts-seed's `typeof import()` type queries are missing from both graph views, and `importResolution` does not report them. felixle's counts are undercounts.
- **No `node_modules`**, so package base types are not in the graph (mustodon's `@nestjs/cqrs` AggregateRoot, for example).
- **Weak style claims.** geekyants and ngduc claim their style by folder structure only; staart and bank-server by framework.
- **No labels yet.** Nothing in §5 is precision, recall or meaningfulness.
- **One run per project**, plus the scratch Neo4j repeat used for the engine-parity check.
