## Statement (one paragraph)

This thesis shows that an architectural specification written once, in a declarative three-layer language, can be
compiled into deterministic queries over a property graph of a TypeScript project, and that these queries can act as
a gate on code generated by language-model agents. The contribution has three parts. First, a pre-registered
experiment (Study 1) finds that the specification given to the generator changes the architecture score of the
generated code, in the direction opposite to the one expected: projects generated with no specification score best.
The effect runs through project size, because a specification makes the generator build more modules and layers on
which the coupling rules can fire. On the one size-matched comparison, the architecture-as-code specification scores
better than the prose specification. Second, the gate's findings are shown
to be accurate. On seeded defects held out under the registration, recall is 0.893 under the registered strict
rule. Every blocking finding on fifteen human-written repositories is correct by a deterministic check that includes
a negative control. On the rules that established tools can also express, the gate agrees with three independent
tools. Third, a language-model judge is given a bounded role beside the deterministic rules: it catches most of the
seeded defects that the rules cannot see, and its limits are reported, including a null result on vote unanimity.
The tooling that made these measurements possible (agent tools over MCP, the in-process query interpreter, reports)
is engineering in support of the contribution and is not claimed as a contribution in itself.

## Wording constraints for the chapters

- **F2 needs its qualifiers in the same sentence.** "Correct" means that the claimed dependency or cycle exists in
  the code. It does not mean that the finding is architecturally meaningful. The fifteen human-written repositories
  are clustered: three of them hold 214 of the 283 findings (PR #167).
- **F3 is development-set evidence.** The rubric was tuned on part of the items, and judge v2.2 was NOT CANDIDATE
  on CHECK-2 (PR #191). The claim is a description of the judge's role, not a validity estimate.
- **The v1.3 hold-out has not been run.** Until it runs (gap G3), any v1.3 figure in the statement carries the
  development or external label given in the ledger. The SO4 figure in F2 belongs to the registered v1.2E study.
- **Study 2 is not part of the statement.** If it runs and reports, the author may add it as a bounded extension of
  F1 (gap G4).
